§1 — Identity

The sender

Every message comes from one no-reply address on the college's own domain. People can't reply to it, but a reply someone sends anyway still reaches a person.

This is an automated message from the College Procurement & Approval System. Please do not reply. For help: procurement-help@college-domain · Request REQ-2026-0001 · Generated 11 Sep 2026, 14:02 IST

  • From is a no-reply mailbox on the college domain, never a personal address. Mail sent "from" the college domain through another provider's servers is what spam filters reject, so the domain's DNS records have to authorise the sender (§7).
  • Reply-To points at a help mailbox someone reads. People reply to automated mail no matter what the footer says; this way the reply reaches a person instead of bouncing.
  • Auto-Submitted: auto-generated (RFC 3834) and X-Auto-Response-Suppress stop out-of-office replies. Without them, an approver's holiday auto-reply goes back to the no-reply mailbox with every notification.
  • Threading. Every email about one request shares a References root, so Gmail and Outlook group REQ-2026-0001's submission, escalation and decision into one conversation.
  • Amounts use Indian grouping — ₹6,50,000.50, not ₹650,000.50 — through Intl.NumberFormat('en-IN'). The financial year replaces the academic year everywhere (§3).
§2 — The rule that shapes everything

Never send mid-approval

The document requires that a mail failure leave the workflow unaffected. That rules out sending email from inside the approval itself.

Why not send straight from the API

Sending inside fn_record_action's transaction fails both ways. If the mail server is slow, the approver waits and the request stays locked. If the approval fails after the email has gone, people are told about a decision that never happened. Email can't be taken back.

Instead the approval writes a record that an email is owed, in the same transaction as the decision. That record only exists if the decision commits. A separate worker process sends from those records afterwards, at its own pace, retrying on its own.

1 · IN THE APPROVALEvent recordedA trigger writes a workflow event beside the decision. Rolled back together.
2 · WORKERRecipients resolvedFrom stage staffing and roles, never stored addresses.
3 · WORKEREmail renderedTemplate filled from the committed state, frozen into the outbox.
4 · WORKERSent over SMTPRetried with backoff on temporary failures.
5 · WORKEROutcome recordedSent, or failed and reported to the administrators.

This is the architecture chain from §14 of the email document — workflow event → stage → roles → users → recipient rules → template → send → notification record → audit log — with one change: the first step is inside the transaction and the rest are outside it. That split is what makes it safe.

§3 — Read this before building

Where the document and the system disagree

The email document was written against the design notes, not the running system. These are the places where building it as written would send wrong or duplicate mail.

Does a lower stage's approval finish the request, or pass it up?

Document"Approved by Purchase Committee … forwarded to the Principal." The procurement design doc's own timeline shows a ₹42,000 request approved by the Committee, then by the Principal.
System todayRequests enter at the stage their amount belongs to, and an approval there is final. Templates 2, 3, 6 and 13 would announce a next step that never happens. Decision A.

Not every request starts at the Purchase Committee

DocumentStage 1 is always Head → Purchase Committee, so "Request Submitted" always goes to the Committee.
PlanThe first email follows the entry stage: Committee for under ₹50,000, the Principal's "approval required" for ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh, "CDC review required" for ₹5 lakh and above.

Academic year

DocumentUses {{academic_year}} throughout, and carry-forward moves a request "to academic year …".
PlanThe system tracks the financial year only. The variable is dropped, and carry-forward says "Carried forward to FY 2027-28".

Two emails to the same people at once

DocumentA Principal escalation sends both "Principal escalated" and "CDC review required" — to the same CDC members. CDC escalation does the same to the final authority.
PlanSend one email per escalation, combining both: who escalated and why, and what is now required.

Copying people who were never involved

DocumentFinal approval goes to the requester, Head, Principal, the Purchase Committee and CDC. It also says "do not automatically CC every person who previously participated".
PlanStage groups are included only if the request actually passed through that stage. A ₹6 lakh request never reached the Committee, so the Committee isn't told of its approval.

The Head of Department can't be identified

Document{{head_email}} is copied on 18 templates.
System todayUsers aren't linked to departments and departments have no head, so there's no way to find the right Head. Omitted until the data exists. Decision C.

"Digital signature recorded"

DocumentEmails each approver confirming their digital signature after every decision.
PlanWhat is recorded is a server-side seal, not the approver's own signature, and the approver already saw the confirmation on screen. Off by default; if kept, reworded to "decision recorded". Decision E.

Two meanings of "issue"

Document"Principal raised issue" is a concern about a procurement request, sent to CDC.
PlanIn the system, "issues" are faculty complaints, which are a separate workflow. Template 10 is sent when the Principal comments on a request that's with CDC. Faculty issues aren't in the document at all. Decision G.

§5 — Who gets what

Choosing recipients

The document's rule: To is whoever must act, Cc is whoever needs to know, and addresses are never hard-coded. The resolver works this out when each email is sent.

EventToCc
Enters a stageEveryone staffed at that stageThe requester; the stage that escalated it
Approved · partial · rejectedThe requesterThe Principal where the request passed through or above them; the deciding stage's other members
Final decisionThe requester, the PrincipalStages the request actually passed through
CommentEveryone permitted to see it (below)—
ReminderCurrent stage—
Carried forwardThe requesterThe Principal
Mail failureAdministrators—

Rules applied to every email

  • Staffing comes from the database, not config files. Adding a CDC member to stage_approvers is all it takes for them to start receiving CDC mail.
  • One copy per person. Someone who is in both To and Cc, or holds two roles, gets a single email, under To.
  • Not about your own action. The approver who just decided isn't emailed that they decided.
  • Disabled accounts receive nothing.
  • One message per event, not one per person. The To/Cc model only works if people can see who else was told. With several action-takers, the greeting names the group ("Dear CDC members"); with one, it names the person.

Recommended

Comment recipients are decided by the same rule as the screen

A restricted comment must only reach people allowed to read it. Rather than rewrite the visibility rule in the mail worker — where it would slowly drift from the real one — the worker checks each candidate by asking the database, as that person, whether they can see the comment. The Row-Level Security policy that hides a CDC note from the requester on screen is the same one that keeps it out of the requester's inbox.

§6 — Content

Writing the emails

The document's {{variable}} syntax happens to be Handlebars syntax, which is also the safest choice here.

  • Handlebars escapes by default. Request titles, rejection reasons and remarks are typed by users. A title containing <a href=…> must appear as text, not become a link in the Principal's inbox. {{title}} escapes it; nothing uses the unescaped {{{…}}} form.
  • Subjects are stripped of line breaks. A title with an embedded newline must not be able to inject extra mail headers.
  • One layout, many bodies. A shared HTML frame (logo, footer, button style) and a plain-text version of every email, as the document requires. Table-based HTML with inline styles — the only layout that survives Outlook.
  • Partial approvals show three figures. Requested → approved → unapproved, for both quantity and amount, as the document's §9 insists. The API already returns unapprovedQuantity, and the original request is never overwritten, so this comes straight from stored data.
  • Deep links need frontend routes. The button goes to {{app_base_url}}/requests/:id. The current frontend switches pages in state and has no URLs, so these links have nowhere to land until it gains routes. They never carry sign-in tokens; the recipient signs in as usual.
  • Frozen at render time. The rendered subject and body are stored before sending, so a retry sends exactly what was first rendered, and the outbox is a record of what people were told.
§7 — Delivery

Mail provider and DNS

The code speaks plain SMTP through Nodemailer, so the provider is a configuration choice. The right one depends on what email the college already uses.

If the college usesSend throughNotes
Google WorkspaceWorkspace SMTP relayMail comes from the college's own domain; Workspace sending limits apply — ample for one college's approvals
Microsoft 365Exchange OnlineMicrosoft is retiring password-based SMTP sign-in; plan on OAuth or a connector-based relay, and check current status with college IT
Neither, or a separate service is preferredA transactional service — Zoho ZeptoMail or Amazon SES (Mumbai region)Built for exactly this traffic, with delivery logs. SES accounts start in a sandbox until production access is granted
Development, on your machineMailpit in DockerCatches every message and shows it in a local web inbox; nothing leaves the laptop

DNS records the college's IT must add

Gmail and Outlook increasingly reject mail from domains that don't authenticate it. Whichever provider is chosen, it will supply exact values for all three:

  • SPF — lists the servers allowed to send as the domain.
  • DKIM — the provider signs each message; the public key lives in DNS.
  • DMARC — tells receivers what to do with mail that fails the first two. Start at p=none with reporting turned on, and tighten to quarantine once reports show only legitimate mail.

Two dependencies outside this project

The college domain itself is still to be finalised (an open question in the requirements document), and adding DNS records needs whoever controls it. Neither blocks building and testing against Mailpit, but both block real delivery.

§8 — Storage

Database additions

Two tables and one role, added as a migration like every change since the first deployment. 

-- Written by triggers inside the approval transaction. Exists only if the decision commits.
CREATE TABLE workflow_events (
    event_id      BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    event_type    TEXT NOT NULL,            -- 'PRINCIPAL_ESCALATE', 'COMMENT_ADDED', ...
    request_id    UUID REFERENCES requests,
    action_id     UUID REFERENCES approval_actions,
    comment_id    UUID REFERENCES comments,
    actor_user_id UUID REFERENCES users,
    occurred_at   TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
    processed_at  TIMESTAMPTZ                -- set once the worker has queued its email
);

-- One row per message. The rendered content is frozen here before sending.
CREATE TABLE email_outbox (
    email_id        UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    event_id        BIGINT REFERENCES workflow_events,
    template_id     TEXT NOT NULL,
    to_addresses    TEXT[] NOT NULL,
    cc_addresses    TEXT[] NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}',
    subject         TEXT NOT NULL,
    html_body       TEXT NOT NULL,
    text_body       TEXT NOT NULL,
    status          TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'PENDING',  -- PENDING, SENT, FAILED, SKIPPED
    attempts        INT  NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
    next_attempt_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT NOW(),
    last_error      TEXT,
    provider_id     TEXT,
    sent_at         TIMESTAMPTZ,
    UNIQUE (event_id, template_id)             -- an event can never queue the same email twice
);
  • Where events come from. A trigger on approval_actions (the action and the stage it happened at give PRINCIPAL_ESCALATE, CDC_REJECT and so on) and one on comments. The in-app notification triggers stay as they are; email is an additional channel, not a replacement.
  • The worker's own database role. The worker has to read requests across the whole college to address mail, so it can't run under the per-user policies the API uses. It gets a dedicated mail_worker role that can read the workflow tables and write only the outbox. That is narrower than postgres, and it isn't the API's app_user, which keeps refusing to start with any role that bypasses Row-Level Security.
§9 — Sending

The mail worker

A small separate process, npm run mail-worker, running beside the API.

  • Picks up work safely. Every few seconds it claims pending rows with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, so a second worker, or a restart mid-batch, can never send the same email twice.
  • Tells temporary from permanent failures. A 4xx reply or a dropped connection is retried after 1 minute, then 5, 30, 2 hours and 6 hours. A 5xx reply such as "no such mailbox" fails at once. Retrying it only damages the domain's sending reputation.
  • Reports what it couldn't send. A message that fails for good queues SYSTEM_NOTIFICATION_FAILURE to the administrators. That alert is never itself alerted on if it fails, so one outage can't set off a chain of failure emails.
  • Leaves the workflow alone. A failed email changes nothing about the request. In-app notifications have already been created by then and still work.
  • Can be switched off. MAIL_ENABLED=false leaves events queued but unsent; turning it back on sends the backlog.
§10 — Time-based mail

Reminders and overdue

The only emails not triggered by someone's action. The worker checks once an hour and sends what is due.

  • Reminder — pending at one stage for more than 3 days, matching the dashboard's "attention required" panel. To the approvers of that stage, at most once every 3 days per stage visit.
  • Overdue — pending past a longer threshold. The number of days and who else is copied (the next authority, administrators, or both) need deciding (D).
  • Safe across restarts. Each reminder is keyed on request, stage, kind and date, so an hourly check that runs twice, or after a restart, still sends it once.
  • Days are counted in IST, not the server's clock, so "3 days" means what the college means by it.

§13 — For Madhuri Mam and the team

Decisions needed

Each changes what gets sent. A and F matter most.

  1. When a stage approves, is the request finished or passed up? The email document and the procurement timeline say the Committee's approval goes on to the Principal. The system treats approval at the entry stage as final. Affects templates 2, 3, 6, 13 — and possibly the workflow
  2. Must both the Chairman and the Vice Chairman approve? Today either one can decide alone. The document's "required" and "completed approvers" suggest both. Affects templates 17–20 and the workflow
  3. How is a request's Head of Department found? By the requester's department, needing users linked to departments and each department's Head recorded. Affects the Cc on 18 templates
  4. When is an approval overdue, and who is told? Suggested: 7 days, copying administrators. Affects template 23
  5. Keep the "digital signature recorded" email? Recommended: no. Affects template 28
  6. What email does the college use, and what is the domain? Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or something else decides the provider, and the domain decides the no-reply address. Blocks phase 8
  7. Should faculty issues send email too? They aren't in the document. Suggested: in-app only for now. Adds up to three templates
